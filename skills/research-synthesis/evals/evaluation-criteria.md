# Evaluation criteria

Use these criteria to assess whether the synthesis is evidence-led and decision-useful.

## It must

- State the scope and limitations of the supplied research.
- Separate direct evidence from interpretation.
- Group evidence into meaningful themes rather than rephrasing every quote.
- Keep material contradictions and differences between participants visible.
- Assign confidence based on evidence quality, not the apparent attractiveness of an idea.
- Link opportunities and questions back to specific themes.
- Recommend a proportionate next step.
- Avoid implying representativeness when the sample does not support it.

## It must not

- Invent quotes, participant details, motivations, or numerical prevalence.
- Treat requested features as proven needs.
- Treat sales anecdotes or competitor behaviour as customer validation.
- Claim a theme is high confidence from a small or narrow sample.
- Hide counter-evidence to make a cleaner narrative.
- Jump directly from a theme to a detailed solution or roadmap.
- Repeat generic research advice without explaining what specifically needs validating.

## Expected behaviour by example

### 01: Research interviews

The output should identify a likely theme around fragmented retrieval and personal knowledge management.

It should also preserve the distinction between:

- Analysts needing to return to prior research
- Teams needing shared provenance and visibility
- Users resisting another system that requires manual maintenance

A good opportunity is to explore low-effort saving and retrieval with clear source and freshness context. It should not conclude that “folders” or “workspaces” are definitely the answer.

### 02: Mixed evidence

The output should challenge the assumption that a weekly AI summary is the central need.

It should identify:

- Interest in timely, source-linked updates
- Concern about additional noise and generic newsletters
- Stronger evidence for finding current company research than for weekly digests
- Weak evidence from sales feedback

The recommended next step should test event- or follow-based updates against the weekly-digest concept.

### 03: Small sample

The output should treat explainability and control as promising but low-to-medium confidence signals.

It should identify:

- The need to distinguish ranking from improving poor hiring briefs
- The risk of black-box decisions in hiring workflows
- The narrow sample and agency bias

The recommended next step should validate the underlying workflow problem across a broader, more varied set of recruiters and hiring managers before designing ranking functionality.
