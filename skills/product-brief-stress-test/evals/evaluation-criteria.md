# Evaluation criteria

Use these criteria to assess whether the skill output is useful.

## It must

- Identify when a request describes a solution rather than a validated problem.
- Distinguish explicit evidence from assumptions.
- Avoid inventing facts not present in the brief.
- Explain why a missing detail matters.
- Suggest proportionate, practical validation methods.
- Recommend one clear next step rather than an oversized plan.
- Raise relevant trust, privacy, accessibility, regulatory, or safety concerns where the brief warrants them.
- Stay concise and specific.

## It must not

- Produce generic filler such as “conduct user research” without specifying what needs validating.
- Treat competitor activity as proof of customer need.
- Assume AI is valuable simply because it is requested.
- Invent KPIs, customer quotes, technical limitations, or legal requirements.
- Jump into feature design or implementation when the core problem is unclear.
- Give every brief the same answer.

## Expected behaviour by example

### 01: Solution-first brief

The output should challenge:

- “Customers” being too broad
- The absence of a specific decision or workflow
- The unsupported assumption that an AI dashboard is the right intervention
- “Higher engagement” being a weak success measure
- Competitor features not being evidence of demand

The recommended next step should be targeted discovery into the reporting workflow and decisions customers struggle to make.

### 02: Incomplete brief

The output should recognise that there is meaningful evidence and a clear user group, while identifying gaps such as:

- Which research artefacts must be saved
- Collaboration and permission expectations
- How to distinguish a workspace from bookmarks or notes
- Whether repeated searching is a retrieval, organisation, or sharing problem
- Measures that define “less time” or “share more often”

The recommended next step should be focused scoping or workflow validation, not broad discovery from scratch.

### 03: High-risk AI feature

The output should flag:

- The need to distinguish education from regulated advice
- Data quality, freshness, and explainability
- User control and safe failure states
- The risk of automated trade acceptance
- The mismatch between success measures and customer benefit
- Competitor activity as weak evidence

The recommended next step should be legal/compliance and problem validation before any automated recommendation design.
