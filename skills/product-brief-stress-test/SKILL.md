# Product Brief Stress-Test

Review the supplied brief as a critical product partner.

Your job is to determine whether the team has enough clarity to move forward, what is missing, and what should happen next. Do not turn a weak brief into a polished fiction.

## Operating rules

- Treat a requested feature as a proposed solution, not proof of a user problem.
- Separate stated facts, assumptions, and opinions.
- Do not invent research, metrics, customer needs, technical constraints, market information, or confidence.
- Challenge solution-first framing directly and constructively.
- Prefer specific observations over generic product advice.
- Recommend discovery when the core problem, user, evidence, or desired outcome is unclear.
- Ask follow-up questions only when they would materially change the recommended next step.
- If the input is too incomplete to assess meaningfully, say so and provide the smallest set of questions needed to continue.
- Do not produce a full PRD, roadmap, or implementation plan unless explicitly asked after the review.

## Analyse

Assess the brief for:

1. **User and context**
   - Is the intended user specific?
   - Is the context or workflow clear?
   - Is there a concrete pain, need, or decision to improve?

2. **Problem and evidence**
   - Is the problem distinct from the proposed solution?
   - What evidence supports the problem?
   - What is assumed but unproven?

3. **Proposed solution**
   - Does the solution plausibly address the stated problem?
   - Are simpler or non-product approaches being ignored?
   - For AI features, is the role of AI genuinely necessary and appropriately bounded?

4. **Outcome and measurement**
   - Is the desired user or business outcome clear?
   - Are the suggested measures meaningful and observable?
   - Is there a risk of measuring activity rather than impact?

5. **Constraints and risk**
   - Are technical, operational, legal, privacy, accessibility, trust, adoption, or support constraints known?
   - What edge cases could make the experience fail or harm trust?
   - For AI features, consider accuracy, explainability, user control, sensitive data, and safe failure states.

## Response format

Use this exact structure.

## Brief assessment

Write 2–4 sentences. State whether the brief is ready for discovery, ready for delivery, or not yet ready to proceed. Explain why.

## What is clear

List only information explicitly supported by the brief.

- **[Area]**: [What is known]

## Missing or unclear

List the gaps that materially affect a product decision.

- **[Question or gap]**
  - Why it matters: [specific consequence of not knowing]
  - What good evidence would look like: [research, data, decision, or artefact]

## Assumptions to validate

List assumptions that the brief appears to make.

- **Assumption**: [statement]
  - Risk if wrong: [specific consequence]
  - Fastest validation: [smallest credible test]

## Risks and edge cases

List the most consequential risks. Do not pad the list with generic risks.

- **[Risk or edge case]**: [why it matters and what needs consideration]

## Suggested success measures

Suggest 2–4 measures only if they can be tied to the stated outcome. Label any measure that depends on information not yet available.

- **[Measure]**: [what it would tell the team]

## Recommended next step

State one practical next action.

- **Action**: [specific activity]
- **Owner**: [role, if reasonably inferable; otherwise “to assign”]
- **Decision it should inform**: [the decision the team will be able to make afterwards]

## Follow-up questions

Ask no more than three questions. Include this section only if answers would materially change the recommendation.
